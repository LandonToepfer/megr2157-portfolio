## Lessons Learned  
Governing failure mode: The two ways of analyzing this bracket gave wildly different results. Taking just feature A as an example, strength gave a radius of .812 inches while stiffness almost doubled at 1.400 inches. They did not have a close measurement anywhere except for where the gap for the beam was. The beam didn't change so the slot made for it didn't either. The stiffness seemed to give bigger numbers where a cantilever was like at the bottom at feature A and B, but everywhere else, stiffness gave smaller numbers. This means that strength would govern most of my measurements.  
Error propagation: My thickness of feature was used in every feature C, D and E. This is where a mistake could cause the greatest error. I made many mistakes but it was mostly along the way of simply just making variables disappear when rearranging equations. This is because I was very tired when doing this assignment and the only thing that caught a lot of those little mistakes was my study buddy who was working on his assignment alongside. Having another person to check over my equations is my foolproof strategy to mitigate my clear mistakes. Sort of like how generative AI will just hallucinate falsehoods, I will just make variables nonexistent from one step to the next.  
Assumption Sensitivity: I actually made the assumption that feature E was an axial loaded model before getting my email back from my TA. This just changed the fact that now the distributed load had a support on the side. Changing that model wildly corrected my numbers as I had 3-4 times larger numbers than my final answers. This just shows that getting your modeling wrong can really throw off your numbers because you wouldn't be using the same formulas to solve.
