**Task B**  
Analyze a physical mechanical product  
&emsp;For this I chose a simple spring loaded clamp. It has three different components, the clamp halves, the spring, and the pivot joint. It's primary purpose is to transfer spring tension into a clamping force applied to an object placed between its jaws, primarily used to secure two objects together. The clamp has two ends, the jaw end which would be driven closed by the spring and the handle end which would be driven open by the spring. The handle end can have a force applied to it in order to exceed the spring force and drive the rotation of the pivot joint in the opposite direction of the spring force. This would open the jaw end for objects to be inserted between the clamp heads before the external force would be released and the clamp heads to be squeezed shut.  

&emsp;This primary mechanism is driven by moments and static equilibrium. The moment equation $'\sum{M} = F * d '$ Moment equals Force times Distance is used to model the rotation of the pivot joint. The distance would be the distance the spring makes contact with the clamp or where an external force is applied near the handle end of the clamp. The force would include the spring force and the external force. The resulting moment would be the addition of the opposing moments caused by the external force and internal spring force which would create rotational motion in the mechanism. Static equilibrium would occur when there is no external force being applied and the two halves of the clamp have squeezed shut. This would mean that the forces from the torsion spring and being cancelled out by the resistive contact forces of the jaws or an object between the jaws. Therefore: $' \sum{F} = 0 '$ and $' \sum{M} = 0 '$. The assumption I just made would be that the body of the clamp was strong enough that when the opposing forces of each half of the clamp cancel out that the frame's deformation is so low that its negligible and can be ignored. This would be a rigid-body assumption that is easy to make because of the scenario where if the body was deforming significantly then the product would be faulty.

&emsp;As you can see above the clamp has three different components, the clamp halves, the pivot joint and the spring. The clamp halves are designed to be longer on one end than the other. The handle end is longer due to the moment equation where you can generate a bigger moment if the force is further away from the pivot joint. This allows the clamp to be stronger and still provide enough torque to the user so that the average user can exceed the spring force and operate the clamp. The pivot joint itself, in tandem with the clamp halves, was purposefully off center so that the torques for either half match their purpose previously stated. The spring is located in the center enveloping the pivot axle so that the spring can be used in a different way than usual. Usually we use a spring to resist compression or tension but here it is a torsion spring used to resist rotation so the central location was imperative to its simple design.  

&emsp;The patent I found closest to my reference product was "Spring Clamp" with patent number "US4813107A". The inventor Vincent B. Cetrone patented it in 1987 and it was licensed to Warren Tool Corporation. This specific patent didn't use a coiled spring but a tensioned piece of material nonetheless. This product specifically could be replaced with other similar inventions depending on the use case. It could be replaced by a clothes pin which is a very similar product that is just usually smaller and made of wood. A clothes pin also clamps an object between the jaws with the main difference being how soft a wooden clothes pin is and the strength needed to hold cloth. It could also be replaced by a bar clamp if the application is woodworking which they are often used for. A bar clamp also clamps an object between it's jaws with the main difference being the capacity or distance between the jaws being much greater than a scissor styles clamp where the capacity is constricted by the central pivot. One feature of my chosen product that separates it from the rest would be the strength of the clamp that might be needed to guarantee that something does not detach as clamps like these are often used on car battery terminals where the application is less domestic like a clothes pin and would need to be more reliable and stronger.
