## Analyze
I analyzed two portfolios based out of GitHub.  
The first one I analyzed was Nathan Hoong's found here https://nhoong.github.io/index.html. His portfolio page felt very spacious due to the large boxes contrasted with small text. It was easy to find all of his projects he worked on as they each had a panel with a title, picture, and a description. Some of the projects were simple CAD designs so they only had a picture and a description. What seemed to be his more complex project did have a button linking to a full report which was detailed and complete. That report could easily be used to recreate his capstone project. Moving back to his main portfolio page, he clearly stated the objective and result for every single project. Some of the more complex CAD designs had multiple pictures with descriptions which helped bridge the gap between objective and result by allowing all of the features to be seen. Overall, his portfolio was very impressive and professional because of the concise language and neutral color pallet.  
The second one I analyzed was Frederick Wachter's found here https://fwachter.github.io. His portfolio page felt very professional, almost classy due to the background chosen and custom logo. His side scrolling page format made it easy to feel like I can see everything he has to offer or would need as a user. His projects page had a title and picture for every project and had a description when hovered over as well as links to GitHub pages, YouTube videos, and documents. Not everything listed had a disclosed report but the ones that did were professional and detailed. His report for his Swerve robot could be easily understood and could be used to recreate his project. The report itself made it very clear what the objective was and who the audience of the project was before he moved onto the individual building steps on how the team reached the final product. Overall, his portfolio felt full due to the amount of projects listed. It was professional due to the concise language and the fact that it did more showing than talking.
